### What changed since the baseline (final, frozen evaluation)

"This run" is the **mean of runs 1 and 2** (N = 2). A third run was recorded but cut short
by Groq's free-tier limit of 200,000 tokens per day for `gpt-oss-120b`: 33 of its 50
questions got no LLM answer. It is kept in the raw JSON and left out of every number here.

**Measurement fixes — not system improvements.** The middle column re-scores the
baseline's own answers: the 12-hour-time fix in the key-fact check (`fix(eval)`, 95c1fea)
and the key facts of a05, a07, a12 (see the dataset changelog). Only the first changes a
baseline score (a34): +2.8 pp answer accuracy. Nothing the system outputs changed.

**System fix.** `fix(rag_core)` (dc077b1) accepts `【1】`-style citation markers that
`gpt-oss-120b` often writes; before, those correct answers were discarded as uncited.
That explains the drop in false refusals (25.0% → 2.8%) and most of the accuracy gain.

**Read with care:**

- *Hallucination rate 3.5% ± 5.0 pp* is one answer in 28 (n13 "Can I pay with Bitcoin?"
  in run 2: "No, Bitcoin is not accepted … [1]"). n13 is a disputed label (dataset
  changelog). The baseline's 0% came from a single run.
- *Answer accuracy 95.8% ± 2.0 pp* is a lexical key-fact check. a12 in run 1 answered
  correctly ("вернуть нельзя") but failed the key fact "не подлеж": a remaining measurement
  limit, left as is because the evaluation is frozen.
- *a03* (12:00 order → same-day delivery) fails in both runs: the delivery document is not
  retrieved. Known retrieval weakness, not fixed on purpose.
- With N = 2 the SD is a rough indication of run-to-run noise, not a confidence interval.
